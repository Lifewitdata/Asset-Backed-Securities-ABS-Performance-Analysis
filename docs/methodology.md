# Research methodology and limitations

## Dataset and provenance
All inputs are generated by `abs_lab.data.generate(seed=42)`. There are 12 amortizing pools (four each labelled Auto, Equipment and Consumer), two notes per pool, and 73 monthly snapshots from December 2019 to December 2025. Sector names are diversification labels; collateral cash-flow mechanics are identical across sectors. Ratings are assigned simulation labels, not rating-agency opinions. This is a fixed cohort with no issuance or survivor selection. The generator deliberately inserts one exact duplicate. Cleaning removes exact duplicate rows and rejects conflicting duplicate keys, invalid rates, nonfinite data, missing months, unknown securities and accounting failures. There are no client records or third-party licensed datasets.

## Cash-flow mechanics
Units are dollars; rates are annual decimals. Each pool starts with 80% senior A and 20% junior B debt, no principal equity. Asset coupon is 10%; servicing fees are 20bp per year. Initial maturity is 120 months. Scheduled principal is straight-line balance divided by remaining months, not an annuity payment. SMM = 1 − (1 − CPR)^(1/12); monthly default probability = 1 − (1 − CDR)^(1/12).

Each month applies scheduled principal, defaults on remaining principal, then voluntary prepayments on survivors. Recovery is an immediate 40% of defaulted principal. Interest is calculated on opening performing proxy balance, without day-count adjustments. Fees precede A interest then B interest. Surplus interest goes to unmodelled residual equity. Principal cash (scheduled + prepaid + recoveries) pays A then B; credit loss writes down B then A. Unpaid coupon is reported as shortfall and not carried forward. No reserve, overcollateralization, excess-spread cure, servicing advance, trigger or legal waterfall is modelled. Ending asset balance equals ending note balances. Each note satisfies opening balance − principal paid − losses = closing balance to one cent, with **zero relative tolerance**.

## Valuation and risk
At each month-end the remaining pool is projected with that date's CPR/CDR held constant to maturity. Future coupon and principal cash are discounted at an annual effective base rate plus a simulated tranche spread. `market_value` is a model present value, not a traded quote. Yield is the discount input, not realised return or option-adjusted spread. Monthly total return = (closing market value + interest received + principal received) / opening market value − 1. Loss is reflected in closing value and is not subtracted twice.

Duration and convexity use symmetric one-basis-point yield bumps **holding projected cash flows fixed**. They are not option-adjusted effective duration/convexity. WAL is the average time of principal actually projected to be paid, divided by total projected principal receipts; it excludes written-off principal. Scenario repricing independently changes CPR, CDR and/or discount yield. Changes in prepayment when rates fall are scenario assumptions, not an estimated borrower-behaviour model. There is no term-structure calibration or OAS calculation.

## Illustrative ABS index
The index is an original demonstration benchmark, not a commercial index replication. Eligibility at each prior month-end: simulated AAA/BBB rating, remaining balance above $1m, and positive model market value. Eligible market values determine next month's weights. Reconstitution and rebalance occur monthly; no future date is used for eligibility or weighting. All cash distributions are immediately reinvested into the same note's total-return units before the next rebalance. No issuance enters this fixed cohort. The index is gross of trading costs.

## Bespoke portfolio construction and manager-style evaluation
Three **rule-based portfolios**, not actual fund managers, are compared with the index:
- Defensive: equal weight in eligible senior notes.
- Income: equal weight in eligible junior notes.
- Minimum variance: CVXPY allocation using trailing 24 monthly returns, long-only, fully invested, at most 15% per note, at most 50% per sector and weighted duration at most four years. A 1e-8 covariance ridge improves numerical conditioning. Constraints are explicit research choices, not suitability recommendations.

The first out-of-sample return is January 2022; the last is December 2025 (48 months). Portfolios pay 10bp times one-way turnover (half the absolute weight change against previous drifted weights); initial investment has turnover one. Cost is deducted from gross portfolio return. This is a simplified proportional-cost convention, not a bid/ask execution model. Infeasible optimization raises an error instead of silently substituting weights.

CAGR is compounded; volatility and tracking error use monthly sample standard deviation times sqrt(12). Drawdown includes the initial investment as a peak. Information ratio uses annualised arithmetic active return divided by tracking error. OLS of strategy returns on index returns reports beta and arithmetic annualised intercept, with HAC standard errors (three lags). These are raw-return regression statistics, not CAPM alpha, and do not establish manager skill. No risk-free return is assumed or Sharpe ratio claimed.

## Time-series modelling
Target: next month's **equal-deal mean annual CDR**. Features at month t: current CDR, one-month lag, trailing three-month CDR mean, current simulated unemployment and base rate. Monthly data is assumed available immediately at month-end; real release delays/revisions would require publication timestamps. OLS (statsmodels) and random forest (scikit-learn) are compared with persistence. Initial training has 36 labelled rows; refitting is expanding-window. A training label is allowed only if its target month is no later than the prediction decision date. Hyperparameters are fixed, not selected using test outcomes. There are 34 forecast observations. Forecasts do not drive the investment backtest.

The seeded run has OLS lowest MAE but persistence lowest RMSE: neither model dominates every metric. This illustrates why a baseline must remain in the report. The small simulated sample cannot establish predictive advantage, scalability or real trading value.

## AI assistance
`python -m abs_lab.ai_brief` exports a prompt and evidence packet without network calls. `--call-api` optionally sends only those simulated aggregate facts to OpenAI, requiring a key and compatible model. Exact typed metric values and allowed keys are validated; explanatory prose still needs human review. A fabricated number in a free-text explanation is not automatically caught. No API response is required for any core result. Live AI calls have not been validated without credentials.

## Scope boundaries
Implemented: demonstrable Python/SQL code, modelling, portfolio construction, index rules, Dash visualization, reporting, tests and a notebook. Not implemented: production servicing ingestion, licensed market prices, legal-deal calibration, live trading, real manager mandates, audited scale benchmarks, authentication, or production monitoring. Projects demonstrate skills; they do not replace the JD's two-year professional Python/RDBMS experience requirement.
